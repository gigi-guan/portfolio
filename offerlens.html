True Purchasing Power

You spent four years in school to land an offer. Most people open a spreadsheet, copy in the numbers, and stare at it. The problem is that nominal salary is a lie. $150K in San Francisco after taxes and rent is materially worse than $120K in Austin. Nobody tells you this clearly, and nobody builds the tool that shows it to you.

I built a cost-of-living adjustment layer utilizing a composite COL index across 18 major cities combined with estimated effective marginal tax rates by state. The output isn't "your salary is $150K". It's "your actual spending power is equivalent to $87K nationally." This was the most important decision in the product because it reframes the entire comparison.

Equity NPV Modeling

A $200K equity grant over 4 years looks the same whether it is at a Series A startup or a public company. It isn't. I built an equity NPV calculator using a 12% discount rate (the standard venture-adjusted rate), modeling a 1-year cliff with monthly vesting thereafter.

This surfaces the time value of equity in a way raw grant numbers never do. I also made the company stage a first-class input, not just because the tool adjusts numbers for it, but because it feeds the AI analysis layer with critical context regarding risk.

The Non-Obvious AI Read

The numbers tell you what. The AI tells you so what. After computing the adjusted figures, the tool passes a structured prompt to Claude that asks for specific, non-generic insights about the user's particular offers. The design requires no login and stores no data, which lowers the trust barrier for purely ephemeral analysis.

The prompt explicitly prohibits repeating numbers verbatim and pushes for the kind of blunt read a sharp mentor would give you. The winner recommendation is intentionally binary with a one-sentence rationale. No hedging, and no "it depends." If it is genuinely close, it says so.
