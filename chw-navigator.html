Case Study · AI Evaluation

CHW Navigator

Building trustworthy AI-assisted healthcare workflows for frontline community health workers.

Led technical validation for an AI-powered clinical decision-support system designed for community health workers in underserved regions. Developed automated testing and evaluation frameworks across 1,000+ simulated patient cases, identifying hidden decision-logic failures and improving the reliability and transparency of system performance.

Project Team Lead · UC Berkeley Haas School of Business

The Problem

Community health workers often lack reliable clinical guidance at the point of care. Extracting decision logic from clinical manuals into deployable models introduces a second risk: systems can look consistent across engines while still being clinically wrong — or agree on metrics that hide untested rules.

The product need was not another demo. It was an evaluation framework that could surface converter defects, misleading agreement scores, and gaps between internal consistency and independently established clinical correctness.

My Role

As Project Team Lead, I owned the technical validation workstream: simulation design, cross-engine evaluation, regression workflows, and coordination of two intern workstreams focused on simulation, testing, and validation.

  • Led technical validation using 1,000+ simulated patients.
  • Developed cross-engine evaluation across JSON, DMN, and XLSForm implementations.
  • Distinguished internal system consistency from independently established clinical correctness.
  • Identified misleading performance metrics and converter/evaluator defects.
  • Built reproducible evaluation and regression testing workflows.

Key Decisions

We treated engine agreement as a consistency signal, not proof of clinical correctness. A separate clinical benchmark grades frozen logic against a WHO 2011 answer-key for referral decisions only — reporting ungradeable patients rather than scoring them as pass or fail.

Rule coverage is surfaced alongside agreement so unexercised logic cannot hide behind a green score. That framing keeps product claims honest: consistency can be measured now; authoritative clinical accuracy still requires an independent oracle.

Results & Impact

1,000+ Simulated patient cases
3 System representations: JSON, DMN, XLSForm
2 Intern workstreams coordinated
Stakeholder Findings shared in presentations involving WHO, UNICEF, and Community Health Toolkit

This work improves reliability and transparency of evaluation. It does not claim that clinical correctness has been fully validated or that the system is medically certified. An authoritative clinical oracle remains necessary for independent accuracy validation.
